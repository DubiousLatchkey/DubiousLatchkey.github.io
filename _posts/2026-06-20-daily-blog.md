## A Manageable Number

Ok, so with restrictions on my spell design, I sat down to actually figure out some encounter design to test builds and I realized I didn't have anything planned for enemies.  I had avoided complex spells in the past to avoid having to write sort of bespoke ai systems for each spell, but I've become a better coder since 2019, so I'm not worried about that anymore.

Anyway, designing encounters means designing enemies.  I could have just thrown some stuff together, but I actually want enemies to have their own thematic identities.  Of course, if I'm going to do top down design, that means I have to actually go back and brush up the story.  I was always dissatisfied with the first act as it was the most magic dependent and also felt forced.  The beginning needs to have the protaganist as a commoner with nothing and end with having a small organization for the rest of the story to work.  I had used magic as a crutch before to write sort of deux ex machina feeling stuff where it was always like "oh and they had this magic knowledge that lets them beat the enemy".  Nah, no more of that, I'm unifying the feel with the rest of the acts and making it more conventional.

After the story tweaks, I was able to divide the game into its main enemy portions.  I want each group to have a thematic identity.  This way, the story portions feel distinct and you get a sense of each kind of enemy.  The earlygame comprises mostly of bandits and nobodies, so they get either no spells or just basic direct damage as a way to ease the player into the game.  In the midgame, there are 3 different lords that end up opposing the main character for various reasons, and, what do you know, there are 3 colors of magic.  Nice how that works out sometimes.  Finally, for the endgame, the enemies are elite mercenaries and the mage guild members themselves.  With these guys being pros, I can give them actual builds with the final enemies having similar toolkits as the player with board manipulation -> payoff spells.  It will take specific ai behavior to make them feel smart, but I have ideas.

With the scope of the encounters done, this, in turn, helps me bring down the potential spell designs to what is needed for these enemies and for the player to get against them in each form.  Now I can start encounter design.  While I'm on that, I'm probably also going to start revisiting the dialogue system.  Things are coming across quite quickly, especially with AI tools now, so I'll probably get to the polish and writing part much faster than I would have estimated before they were around.
